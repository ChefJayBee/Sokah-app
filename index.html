Menu

Every plate you have saved: what it costs, what it sells for, and the food cost % that gives. Setting a price here is the deliberate step, with your name on it.

Approved price
The price you set here, newest first. Old prices stay as history. Recipes use it for food cost % unless you are trying a different price in step 3.
Suggested
Food per portion ÷ the plate's target, rounded up to $0.50. An idea, not a change.
Food cost %
Food per portion ÷ approved price. Green at or under target, amber under 35%, red above.
Sides
A side marked "goes into other recipes" is not listed; it is priced through the plates that use it. A side you also sell on its own: mark it "sold as a plate" and price it here.

How this works · 4 steps

  1. Name it. The recipe, how much one batch makes, and how big one portion is.
  2. Add what goes in. For each ingredient: the amount you use, what you paid, and how much of it is usable after trim.
  3. Read the numbers. The bar at the bottom updates as you type: batch cost, cost per portion, a suggested price, and your food cost %.
  4. Save it. Saved recipes stay on this page. Open one later, copy it, set its price on Menu, or send it to the workbook.

"Sold as a plate" = a customer buys it, so it gets pricing. "Goes into other recipes" = a sauce, rub or side that feeds plates; it only needs a cost per portion, no price.

What you call it on the line. "House BBQ sauce", "Mac & cheese".
Batch makes
How much one batch gives you, in whatever you measure it in: 16 portions, 5 lb, 100 fl oz.
Portion size
How much goes on one plate, in the same unit. Batch ÷ portion = number of portions.
Target food cost %
The share of the price you want food to cost. 32% means a $10 plate should have about $3.20 of food on it.

2Add what goes in

One line per ingredient. Type the amount you actually use; the yield adds the trim you threw away.

What each box means
Ingredient name
Start typing. Your pantry offers matches (open it with the Pantry button up top). Tap one and the price and pack fill in. Not there yet? Type the price here; saving the recipe adds it to the pantry.
Amount you use
What goes in the pot, after peeling and trimming. 2 lb of diced onion is 2 lb here.
Unit
oz and lb are weight. fl oz, cup, qt, gal are volume. Weight and volume never convert into each other.
You paid / for this much
Straight off the invoice: $25 for a 50 lb bag. The page works out the price per lb.
Usable after trim
How much of what you bought you can actually cook with. Whole onions: about 88%. Frozen fries, cheese, butter: 100%.
Yield is
"Standard estimate" = a typical published number. "Weighed by us" = you weighed a case before and after trim. Standard ones show CHECK until you weigh your own.
FOOD / PACKAGING
Tap to switch. Containers and lids count in the cost, but never in food cost %.

3Read the numbers

Nothing to type here. This is what the recipe costs and what it could sell for.

What the numbers mean
Target dial
The food cost % you want this recipe to hit. Slide it or tap a number and the suggested price follows. Each recipe keeps its own target; 32% is the house default.
Food, whole batch
Every food line added up. Packaging is not in here.
Food per portion
Batch food cost ÷ number of portions.
Suggested price
Food per portion ÷ your target %, rounded up to the next $0.50. It is a suggestion. The real price is set on Menu, with your name on it.
Food cost %
Food per portion ÷ the menu price you typed. Green = at or under target. Amber = over target but under 35%. Red = over 35%.
Left after food + packaging
Menu price minus food and packaging. Not profit: labor, rent and card fees still come out of it.
